At 18, Onyx is moving with the urgency of someone who knows exactly what he wants, even if he is still discovering what that vision will become. Also known as Juggbaby77, Onyx77, the North Plainfield, New Jersey-based artist has spent the past year turning years of experimentation into something more intentional. Between *Sunkist*, his debut EP released in April, *Sunset*, his debut album released in July, and his upcoming second album, *Red Moon*, he is beginning to establish an identity that stretches beyond any single sound or scene.

His music moves between melodic vocals, aggressive rage production, nostalgic melodies, and experimental sounds that resist easy categorization. Yet beneath the energy and constantly shifting production is something more personal. For Onyx, music is an outlet, a reflection of his emotions, and a way to communicate parts of himself that might otherwise remain unspoken.

“I like to see the art in everything,” he says, extending that perspective beyond music to video games, films, friendships, and the people around him. It is a philosophy that helps explain why his creative identity is inseparable from the experiences and relationships that have shaped him.

## Finding His Footing

Onyx's story begins between two cultures. With Trinidadian and Cuban roots, he spent some of his early years in Trinidad before eventually settling in New Jersey. Growing up around Carnival bands, music, and dancing introduced him to the energy of performance, although music did not immediately become his primary focus. Soccer occupied much of his attention, and his relationship with music developed more naturally over time.

His upbringing in Trinidad also gave him a different perspective on the world. He believes being there helped soften some of the negativity he might otherwise have encountered growing up in the United States. That early exposure to different environments became part of the foundation he would eventually draw from creatively. With the support of his parents, music gradually became more than an interest, developing into something he wanted to pursue seriously.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/6onyx.JPG" alt="" />
</figure>

He has been making music since around sixth grade, but the distance between making songs and building an artistic identity took time to close. Like many young artists finding their footing online, he spent years exploring sounds, experimenting with different approaches, and figuring out what felt natural to him.

One of the people who helped him recognize what was possible was Vax, an artist he watched develop and someone he credits with helping him find his footing. Seeing that progression gave Onyx a reference point for his own ambitions. Rather than treating music as a distant possibility, he began to see a future he could actively work toward.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/10onyx.JPG" alt="" />
</figure>

His influences also reflect the evolution of the internet's underground music scene. Artists such as Lil Uzi Vert, XXXTentacion, and Smokepurpp helped shape his appreciation for the melodic, unpredictable energy associated with the SoundCloud era. More contemporary artists have expanded his perspective in different directions. He admires Glo's emotional resonance and storytelling, Osamason's experimental approach to vocals and production, and Che's willingness to bring punk, rock, rage, and other sounds together.

What connects these influences is not a single genre but the feeling they create. Onyx pays attention to the way a voice moves across a beat, how production changes the atmosphere of a song, and how an artist can make something immediately recognizable without following an established formula.

That distinction matters to him. The underground can be heavily influenced by trends, but he does not want his music to become an imitation of whoever is popular at a given moment. Even when working with a familiar sound like rage, he approaches it by asking what he can bring to it rather than what another artist would do.

## *Sunkist*: A Year Captured In Music

Released on April 13, 2026, *Sunkist* marked a turning point in Onyx's development. Although he had already been making music for years, the EP represented his first serious commitment to a complete project, one that reflected a specific period of his life rather than simply a collection of individual songs.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/sunkit.jpg"/>
  <figcaption>Sunkist, Onyx's first EP, published April 13th, 2026.</figcaption>
</figure>

The timing made it particularly meaningful. He finished the project around his high school graduation and even played it at prom, bringing the music into the same environment that had helped shape its themes. The EP became a record of his senior year, capturing a period of change, uncertainty, excitement, and personal growth.

Sonically, *Sunkist* allowed him to explore a more melodic direction while working with different producers and experimenting with the sounds available to him. At the time, he had plenty of unreleased music and was spending a lot of time around rage beats. A conversation with a then friend encouraged him to move away from that approach and consider what else he could create.

That shift helped inspire songs such as “Wuhu,” “Peace,” and “Chopstick.” Some of the music came together remarkably quickly, including songs he made shortly before leaving for work. Rather than treating that spontaneity as a limitation, he leaned into it. The immediacy of those sessions helped him capture ideas before overthinking them, allowing the energy of the moment to shape the final result.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/4onyx.JPG"/>
</figure>

His creative process still relies heavily on freestyling. He rarely writes lyrics beforehand, preferring to let the beat guide his flow and the words emerge naturally. He does not want to recycle the same lyrics or recreate the exact energy of an earlier song. For him, every session offers an opportunity to discover something new.

But *Sunkist* was not only about finding a sound. It was also about learning what it meant to release something personal into the world.

The rollout required a level of promotion and commitment that went beyond making music. Seeing listeners respond positively to the project gave him a sense of accomplishment and encouraged him to continue. For someone who had spent years experimenting without necessarily knowing where it would lead, *Sunkist* was proof that his work could connect with people outside his immediate circle.

It also established a foundation for what came next.

## Turning Momentum Into *Sunset*

Released on July 31, 2026, *Sunset* arrived only a few months after *Sunkist*, but the two projects represent different stages of Onyx's development.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/sunset.jpg"/>
  <figcaption>Sunset, Onyx's debut album, published July 31st, 2026.</figcaption>
</figure>

Where the EP captured a concentrated period of his life, the album gave him more room to experiment. He began working on it after *Sunkist* came out, encouraged by the positive feedback and motivated to keep creating. Produced throughout the summer, *Sunset* became a more considered body of work, balancing melodic vocals, energetic production, and a warmer, nostalgic atmosphere.

He describes the album as a summer party record, but not one tied to a particular genre. Instead, he wanted the freedom to experiment and follow whatever direction felt right for each song. Some tracks lean into aggressive rage production, while others prioritize melody and atmosphere. The variety reflects his belief that an artist should not have to remain within a single sonic identity to be recognizable.

His process remained instinctive, although he spent more time developing the material. He still allowed songs to emerge naturally rather than writing everything in advance, but he approached the project with greater awareness of what he wanted to accomplish.

That balance between spontaneity and intention is central to his growth. He wants to preserve the feeling that makes a song exciting in the first place while learning how to build a more complete project around it.

His personal life also played a significant role in reaching this point. Onyx describes spending roughly two years in a difficult relationship that affected his confidence and motivation. During that period, he felt disconnected from himself and was not putting the same amount of work into music. He was around other people creating, but he struggled to find the drive to develop his own craft.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/16onyx.JPG"/>
</figure>

When that chapter ended, his relationship with music changed.

He began dedicating himself to it with a renewed sense of purpose, spending more time learning, experimenting, and developing his sound. The shift was not simply about having more time to make songs. It was about rediscovering a part of himself he felt he had lost.

One of the clearest examples of the personal side of his music is “If I If I.” He connects the song to a period when he was seeking help for his mental and emotional struggles and felt that the treatment being discussed did not fully address what he was experiencing. Around the same time, his relationship with Kiki was beginning to develop, and her presence helped him navigate that period.

The song became a way to capture the emotional significance of that experience. Rather than treating music as separate from his life, he allowed the two to overlap.

For Onyx, that is the point. A song can have an infectious melody or an exciting beat, but its meaning comes from the experiences behind it. The listener may arrive for the sound and discover the story later.

## The People Behind The Sound

Although Onyx's creative identity is deeply personal, his journey has never been entirely solitary. His circle of friends, collaborators, and producers has become an important part of the world he is building. For him, the people around him are more than collaborators; they're part of the reason he continues to create.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/7onyx.JPG"/>
</figure>

One of the most significant figures in his journey is Vax, someone Onyx credits with helping shape the artist he is today. He speaks about him with genuine appreciation, describing him as someone who helped put him on to new sounds, showed him different approaches to music, and encouraged him to recognize his own potential. More than anything, Vax is someone he considers a real friend. Onyx doesn't separate that personal connection from the influence he's had on his development.

His appreciation extends to the wider circle of artists and producers he has connected with along the way. Artists including Twilijht, Acceee, Ainhunna, Dr4co, and 7k, alongside producers such as kvma, Lucid740, Vax, Chrisxo, Viva, FC, BMUD, and Shishastopsippin, have all contributed to the creative environment surrounding him.

One collaboration that stands out is his connection with Shishastopsippin, a Russian producer he discovered through YouTube. After coming across one of his beats, Onyx was immediately drawn to the production. He eventually found Shisha's Telegram and reached out, impressed by the distinct quality of his work. The connection developed into something much bigger, with Onyx eventually putting together an entire EP using his beats.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/5onyx.JPG"/>
</figure>

His relationships outside the studio have been just as influential. Kiki, in particular, has played a significant role in his personal and creative development. He credits her as one of the major reasons *Sunkist* came together, but her influence extends beyond the music itself. Their conversations helped him connect with someone on a deeper level, encouraging him to become more appreciative, open, and intentional about the way he approaches life.

</figure>

For Onyx, the right people don't simply add to your life; they change the way you experience it. He sees love, support, and genuine connection as things that can give someone a different perspective on themselves and the world around them.

That same sense of support is reflected in his friendships with Dior Zack. He speaks highly of the people who believed in his abilities, recognized his potential, and encouraged him to continue pursuing music. He also values being someone who can offer that same support to others, particularly when it comes to giving friends the space and confidence to express themselves creatively.

Kiki has also become a mentor to Dior Zack, sharing her knowledge and helping him develop. For Onyx, seeing those relationships evolve into something resembling a family is part of what makes the community meaningful.

Through Onelife and the friendships surrounding it, he has found an environment where everyone has something to contribute. Whether it's producing, engineering, sharing ideas, or simply listening to a new song together, the collective energy keeps everyone moving.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/14onyx.JPG"/>

He believes that when people are genuinely invested in one another, the possibilities become much greater. If one person struggles, the others are there to help them back up. Everyone has a role to play, and everyone benefits from seeing the people around them succeed.

Ultimately, Onyx wants to build more than a career. He wants to be part of a community where creativity is shared, opportunities are created together, and nobody has to figure everything out alone.

## *Red Moon*: Three Sides Of The Same Artist

On October 31, 2026, Onyx is set to release *Red Moon*, his second album and the next step in a year that has already seen him evolve considerably.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/redmoon.jpg"/>
  <figcaption>Sunkist, Onyx's second album, to be published on October 31st, 2026.</figcaption>
</figure>

He describes the project as unfolding across three distinct phases. The beginning is aggressive, designed to immediately capture the listener's attention. The middle shifts into melodic territory, with a more romantic feel. By the end, the different energies come together, creating a conclusion that brings the project's contrasting moods into the same space.

The structure reflects the versatility he has been developing across his recent releases. Rather than choosing between aggressive production and melodic expression, he wants to demonstrate how both can exist within the same artistic identity.

He considers *Red Moon* among his strongest work to date, placing it alongside *Sunkist* as a personal contender for his best released project. Yet he is careful not to treat any album as a final statement about what he can do.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/11onyx.JPG"/>
</figure>

There are too many experiences left to have and too many sounds left to explore. Every project represents a particular point in his life, and there is always the possibility of creating something that reflects a different version of himself.

That perspective also shapes his ambitions. He wants to build a dedicated fanbase, perform for larger audiences, and eventually establish himself as a hometown hero in New Jersey. He hopes to travel, experience different environments, and allow those experiences to influence his music as he grows.

Financial success matters to him, too. He wants to make a living from his art, invest in quality equipment, work with a strong team, and enjoy a life in which he can create without constantly worrying about money. His ambition is not limited to recognition; it includes the freedom to live comfortably and make music on his own terms.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/15onyx.JPG"/>
</figure>

Still, he does not see his future as a departure from where he started. New Jersey remains important to him because of the people who have supported him and the relationships he has built there. Becoming a hometown hero would mean something precisely because it would connect his growth to the community that helped shape him.

## Making Art That Lasts

In an underground scene where new sounds and trends can emerge almost overnight, Onyx believes patience is one of the most important qualities an artist can develop.

He sees too many people rushing toward recognition before they have fully established their sound. For him, consistency matters, but so does taking the time to understand the craft. Artists need to keep learning, refining their ideas, and pushing themselves beyond what they have already accomplished.

He applies the same philosophy to the way he thinks about authenticity. Music should not feel forced or disconnected from the person making it. He wants to preserve the emotional honesty that first drew him to creating, especially in a landscape where he feels some music has become more about following formulas than expressing something genuine.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/8onyx.JPG"/>
</figure>

His advice extends beyond music. He believes people need to recognize their own value, discover what makes them feel passionate, and stop allowing the expectations of others to determine the boundaries of their lives.

There will always be moments of insecurity, he acknowledges, but those moments should not become reasons to abandon what makes someone different. Learning to appreciate yourself, remaining open to new experiences, and showing love to the people around you are all part of building a life that feels meaningful.

That philosophy comes back to the way he sees art. Music is one expression of it, but so are the relationships that sustain him, the friends who celebrate his accomplishments, and the experiences that change how he understands himself.

For Onyx, the ambition is to become a legend, but the work is much more immediate: keep creating, keep learning, and keep finding new ways to turn his experiences into something other people can feel.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/9onyx.JPG"/>
</figure>

His music is still developing, and he knows there is much more ahead of him. *Sunkist* captured a chapter of his life. *Sunset* showed him what he could do with the momentum that followed. *Red Moon* represents another opportunity to explore the space between the sounds he loves and the person he continues to become.

He is not interested in being confined to one genre, one moment, or one version of himself. He wants to grow without losing the instinct that made him start creating in the first place.

“My music is my feelings,” he says.

For an artist still at the beginning of his journey, that may be the clearest explanation of what he is trying to build: a sound that can change as he does, while remaining connected to the person behind it.
